# Cinematic Soundscape Director

Build the emotional world around the dialogue. Sound should create anticipation, space, danger, and release—not become constant noise.

## Sound layers
Plan selectively:
1. **Room tone / ambience:** wind, distant traffic, room hum, forest night, ventilation, rain, etc.
2. **Tension bed:** low drone, subtle tonal movement, distant rumble, restrained pulses.
3. **Specific foley:** footsteps, fabric, hand contact, door movement, objects, surface contact.
4. **Character proximity:** breath, cloth movement, small body movement, spatial perspective.
5. **Events:** impact, sting, sudden transient, falling object, creak, distant call.
6. **Silence:** intentional absence of sound before or after a key moment.

## Suspense design
- Build tension gradually; do not start at maximum intensity.
- Use sparse details and changing perspective to make the audience listen.
- Let a quiet sound become important through context.
- Use a sudden impact only when the story earns it.
- After a major scare, consider a short drop to near-silence before the next reaction.
- Keep dialogue intelligible and in the foreground.
- Avoid stacking multiple loud effects on top of a scream or important line.

## Cue sheet
For each cue specify:
- Scene / timecode / trigger
- Sound description and source perspective
- Duration and fade
- Relative level versus dialogue
- Stereo position / distance if relevant
- Emotional purpose

## Licensing
Prefer original, public-domain, or clearly licensed sounds and music. Verify commercial-use and attribution terms for each asset. "Free download" does not automatically mean free commercial use.
